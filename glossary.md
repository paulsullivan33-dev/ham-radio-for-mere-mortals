# Glossary

Every jargon term used in this guide, in one place.

- **Activator** — in POTA/SOTA, the operator transmitting from
  the park or summit.
- **Amateur Radio Service** — the formal FCC name for ham
  radio: a licensed, non-commercial radio service for
  individuals.
- **ARES (Amateur Radio Emergency Service)** — ARRL-organized
  volunteers who provide emergency communications.
- **APRS** — Automatic Packet Reporting System; VHF packet
  system for position reports and short messages.
- **Band** — a range of frequencies allocated to amateur use
  (e.g., the 2-meter band).
- **Band plan** — voluntary convention for where modes and
  activities go within each band.
- **Base station** — a full-featured fixed radio setup,
  typically at a desk.
- **Calling frequency** — an agreed frequency for making
  initial contact before moving elsewhere (146.52 MHz on 2 m,
  446.000 MHz on 70 cm).
- **Callsign** — the unique letter-number identifier the FCC
  issues to each licensed amateur.
- **Chaser / hunter** — in SOTA/POTA, the home station working
  activators.
- **CORES** — the FCC's online registration system; where you
  get your FRN and pay FCC fees.
- **Courtesy beep** — the short tone some repeaters emit when
  a transmission ends.
- **CTCSS / PL** — Continuous Tone-Coded Squelch System
  (Motorola's "Private Line"): a sub-audible tone that unlocks
  a repeater.
- **CW** — Continuous Wave; Morse code transmission.
- **Dipole** — the simplest effective antenna: two wires, end
  to end, fed in the middle.
- **Digipeater** — a station (often on a satellite) that
  receives and retransmits packet data.
- **DX** — distant communication, especially other countries
  or continents.
- **Element 2 / 3 / 4** — the FCC written exams for
  Technician, General, and Extra respectively.
- **FCC** — Federal Communications Commission; the US
  regulator for amateur radio.
- **Feedline** — the cable (usually coaxial) carrying signal
  between radio and antenna.
- **Field Day** — the annual ARRL emergency-preparedness
  exercise (last full weekend of June).
- **FM** — Frequency Modulation; the standard voice mode on
  VHF/UHF.
- **Foldback** — a radio's automatic power reduction when SWR
  is dangerously high.
- **FRN** — FCC Registration Number; your ten-digit FCC ID,
  obtained via CORES.
- **FT8** — a weak-signal digital mode exchanging minimal
  data in 15-second cycles.
- **General / Extra** — the US license classes above
  Technician, each unlocking more frequencies.
- **HF** — High Frequency (3–30 MHz); the shortwave bands
  that bounce off the ionosphere.
- **HT** — handy-talkie; a handheld radio.
- **Input / output** — a repeater's listen frequency and
  transmit frequency.
- **Ionosphere** — electrified upper-atmosphere layers that
  reflect HF signals back to Earth.
- **Kerchunk** — keying a repeater without identifying, just
  to hear the courtesy beep. Don't.
- **MHz** — megahertz; millions of cycles per second, the
  unit of radio frequency.
- **Mobile** — a higher-power radio designed for vehicle (or
  desk) use with an external antenna.
- **Mode** — how information is encoded on the radio wave
  (FM, SSB, CW, digital...).
- **Net** — a scheduled on-air gathering, usually on a
  repeater.
- **Offset** — the gap between a repeater's input and output
  frequencies (±600 kHz on 2 m, ±5 MHz on 70 cm).
- **Packet** — digital data sent over radio in addressed
  bursts.
- **PEP** — Peak Envelope Power; how transmitter power limits
  are measured for SSB.
- **Phonetic alphabet** — the NATO/ICAO word-per-letter
  spelling alphabet (Alfa, Bravo, Charlie...).
- **Pileup** — many stations calling one rare station at
  once, common in DX and contests.
- **PL** — see CTCSS.
- **POTA (Parks on the Air)** — program for portable operation
  from registered parks.
- **PSK31** — a keyboard-to-keyboard digital chat mode.
- **Q-signals** — three-letter codes (QTH, QRM, QSL...)
  inherited from Morse and commercial radio.
- **QSL** — confirmation of a contact; also the cards hams
  exchange to confirm them.
- **RACES (Radio Amateur Civil Emergency Service)** — the
  FCC-defined emergency service activated by civil-defense
  authorities.
- **Ragchew** — an informal extended chat on the air.
- **Repeater** — a high, high-power station that
  retransmits signals to extend range.
- **RF exposure** — FCC rules (§97.13(c)) limiting human
  exposure to radio-frequency energy near antennas.
- **Rubber duck** — the short flexible whip antenna supplied
  with handhelds.
- **RTTY** — radioteletype; an early digital text mode.
- **73 / 88** — ham shorthand for "best regards" / "love and
  kisses."
- **Signal report** — an assessment of how well you're being
  heard (strength, clarity).
- **Simplex** — transmitting and receiving on the same
  frequency, radio to radio.
- **Skywarn** — National Weather Service program training
  volunteer storm spotters (many are hams).
- **SOTA (Summits on the Air)** — program for portable
  operation from registered summits.
- **SSB** — Single Sideband; the efficient voice mode
  standard on HF.
- **SWR** — Standing Wave Ratio; the match quality between
  radio and antenna (1:1 ideal).
- **Technician** — the entry-level US amateur license class.
- **Transponder** — satellite equipment that receives on one
  band and retransmits on another.
- **UHF** — Ultra High Frequency (300–3000 MHz); includes
  the 70 cm band.
- **ULS** — the FCC's Universal Licensing System database,
  where licenses and callsigns are recorded.
- **Vanity callsign** — a callsign you choose (when
  available) instead of the sequentially assigned one.
- **VE** — Volunteer Examiner; an accredited ham who
  administers license exams.
- **VEC** — Volunteer Examiner Coordinator; the organization
  accrediting VEs.
- **VHF** — Very High Frequency (30–300 MHz); includes the
  6 m and 2 m bands.
- **Wavelength** — the physical length of one radio-wave
  cycle; bands are named for it (2 m ≈ 144 MHz).
- **WSJT-X** — free software implementing FT8 and other
  weak-signal digital modes.
- **WSPR** — Weak Signal Propagation Reporter; beacon mode
  for testing propagation paths.
