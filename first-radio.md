# Your First Radio

This chapter deliberately names **no models and no prices**.
Models change yearly, prices change monthly, and what's "best"
depends on your situation. Instead: the three categories, what
each is for, and what to look for.

## The three categories

### Handheld (HT — "handy-talkie")

A walkie-talkie-sized radio, battery-powered, with a short whip
antenna. Typically 5 watts on 2 m and 70 cm, FM only.

- **Best for:** your first radio, full stop. Repeaters, club
  nets, public-service events, hiking. Fits in a jacket pocket.
- **Limits:** 5 watts and a stubby antenna mean you're leaning
  on repeaters. Simplex range is modest. Battery life is hours,
  not days, of heavy use.
- **Honest note:** the cheapest handhelds are *very* cheap and
  work fine for repeater access — but their receivers can be
  overwhelmed near strong signals (a known weakness of
  budget designs). If yours hears interference others don't,
  that's likely why.

### Mobile

A bigger radio meant for a car (or a desk with a power supply),
typically 25–50 watts, with an external antenna.

- **Best for:** your second radio, or your first if you mainly
  want reliable repeater access from home or car. More power and
  a real antenna transform what 2 m/70 cm can do.
- **Needs:** 12-volt power (car battery or a base power supply)
  and an external antenna — a magnetic-mount on the car roof or
  a simple outdoor antenna at home.

### Base / HF station

A full-featured radio for a desk, usually covering HF plus
VHF/UHF, with SSB for long-distance work.

- **Best for:** later, when the HF bug bites (worldwide
  contacts, digital modes).
- **Honest note:** this is where the money goes — radio, power
  supply, antenna system, possibly an antenna tuner. Wonderful
  hobby territory, but not a first purchase for most people.

## What to look for

Whatever category you choose:

- **Bands:** at minimum 2 m and 70 cm (dual-band). Single-band
  2 m radios exist and are fine if your local activity is all
  there — check your area first.
- **Power:** 5 W handheld / 25–50 W mobile is the standard
  useful range. More watts help less than a better antenna.
- **Programming:** you'll enter repeater frequencies, offsets,
  and tones. Radios with computer programming (via cable and
  software) save enormous tedium over punching everything in by
  hand on a keypad. Ask the seller or club what cable/software a
  radio needs.
- **Receiver quality:** matters more than transmit power for
  enjoyment. A deaf radio hears nothing no matter the watts.
- **Used is fine.** Ham radio has a healthy used market
  (hamfests, club mailing lists). A used radio from a known
  manufacturer, tested by the seller, is often the best value
  in the hobby.

## What you don't need yet

An amplifier, a tower, an HF beam antenna, or any accessory
with more knobs than the radio. Start with the radio, learn the
repeaters, join the club. The hobby will tell you what to buy
next — usually an antenna upgrade, not more radio.

> **Check:** Before buying, visit a local club meeting or ask on
> a local repeater net what people in *your area* use. Local
> advice beats any generic recommendation: they'll know which
> repeaters are active, which bands matter locally, and what
> programming help is available.
