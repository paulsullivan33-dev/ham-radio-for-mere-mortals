# Your First Contact

You've got the license, the radio is programmed with the local
repeater, and you've listened for a week. Time to talk. It's
simpler than you're imagining.

## Calling on a repeater

When the repeater is quiet, key the mic, pause half a second
(keying takes a moment to wake the repeater up — starting to
talk instantly clips your first syllable), and say:

> **"KI5ABC, listening."**

Replace KI5ABC with your callsign, in phonetics if you like
("Kilo India Five Alfa Bravo Charlie, listening"). Then
unkey and wait. Someone will usually come back within a minute
or two — hams *like* answering first-timers.

When they reply, they'll give their callsign and name. Answer
with theirs, then yours:

> "Thanks for coming back, N5XYZ. This is KI5ABC — Tom, I'm a
> new Technician, this is my first contact. How's my signal?"

That's a complete first contact. Chat a bit, and when you're
done: "73, N5XYZ, this is KI5ABC clear." (**73** is ham
shorthand for "best regards," pronounced "seven-three.")

## The phonetic alphabet in use

Use phonetics for your callsign the first time in each contact,
and any time someone asks for a repeat. After that, plain
letters are fine on a clear repeater. On noisy simplex or HF,
stay phonetic — that's what it's for.

## What not to do

- **Don't say "over and out."** "Over" means "your turn";
  "out" means "I'm done." Together they're contradictory. Just
  say your callsign when you're finished.
- **Don't use CB lingo.** "10-4," "breaker breaker," "what's
  your 20" — that's a different service. Hams say "my location
  is..." and "QTH" (location) if they want shorthand.
- **Don't apologize for being new.** Nobody minds. Every ham
  remembers their first contact.
- **Don't monopolize.** On a busy repeater, keep first chats
  short — five minutes is plenty. Long conversations belong on
  simplex or a quiet repeater.
- **Don't forget to ID.** End every contact with your callsign
  (§97.119). "KI5ABC clear" covers it.

## A few useful Q-signals

Hams inherited three-letter **Q-signals** from Morse and
commercial radio. You only need a handful for voice:

| Signal | Meaning |
|---|---|
| QTH | My location is... |
| QRM | Interference (from other stations) |
| QRN | Interference (from static/nature) |
| QSB | Your signal is fading |
| QSL | I acknowledge / confirmation (also the name for contact confirmation cards) |
| 73 | Best regards |
| 88 | Love and kisses (used between close friends/family — know what it means before you send it) |

> **Check:** After your first contact, look up the other
> station's callsign in the FCC ULS or a callsign lookup site.
> You'll see their name, license class, and location — and
> they'll do the same for you. Callsigns are public by design.
