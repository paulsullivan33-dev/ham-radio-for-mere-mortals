# Troubleshooting

Most beginner problems are settings, not broken gear. Work
through the symptom that matches.

## "I can't hit the repeater"

The repeater doesn't answer — no courtesy beep, no
retransmission of your audio.

1. **Wrong tone (most common).** Verify the repeater's CTCSS
   tone from a current directory and check your radio is
   *transmitting* it (tone mode "T" or "TONE," not just tone
   squelch on receive). One wrong digit = total silence.
2. **Wrong offset direction.** Confirm plus/minus for the
   repeater and that your radio applied it. A few repeaters
   use non-standard offsets — the directory says so when they
   do.
3. **Out of range.** 5 watts into a rubber-duck antenna from
   inside a building is the weakest possible configuration.
   Step outside, or try from higher ground or a car with an
   external antenna, before concluding anything.
4. **Repeater down.** Repeaters go offline for maintenance.
   Check the club's website or ask on another repeater.

## "Nobody answers my call"

1. **Dead repeater, wrong time.** Some repeaters are quiet for
   hours. Try during commute times or a scheduled net.
2. **You're not actually transmitting.** See below.
3. **They can't understand you.** On simplex or HF, switch to
   full phonetics and speak slowly across the mic (not into
   it — a fist's width away, steady pace).
4. **Wrong frequency/mode.** Double-check you dialed what you
   think you dialed — 146.520 vs. 146.025 is an easy slip.

## "Am I even transmitting?"

1. Watch the radio's transmit indicator (usually a red LED or
   icon) when you key up.
2. Listen on a second receiver if you have one (even a cheap
   scanner): key up on simplex and confirm you hear yourself.
3. Ask for a **signal report** on a net: "KI5ABC, can I get a
   signal check?" Someone will tell you how you sound.
4. Check the battery. A nearly-dead battery can light the
   display but collapse under transmit current.

## "High SWR" (see [Antennas](antennas.md))

1. **Check every connection.** Loose or corroded coax
   connectors are the #1 cause. Disconnect, inspect, reseat.
2. **Right antenna, right band?** Transmitting on 70 cm
   through a 2 m-only antenna gives terrible SWR. Verify the
   antenna covers the frequency you're on.
3. **Something changed near the antenna.** New metal gutter,
   a fallen branch touching the wire, the antenna moved —
   surroundings matter.
4. **Feedline damage.** Kinked, chewed, or waterlogged coax
   misbehaves. If SWR is bad on all bands, suspect the
   feedline.
5. Measure at **low power**. Don't troubleshoot SWR at full
   power — if something is wrong, low power limits the damage.

## "I hear the repeater but it sounds distorted / I hear interference"

1. Budget handheld receivers overload near strong transmitters
   (broadcast towers, pagers). If a better radio at the same
   spot hears fine, your receiver is the limit, not the
   repeater.
2. Try the repeater's input frequency on simplex — if you hear
   someone there, you're hearing another user direct, which is
   normal.

## General method

Change **one thing at a time**, and keep notes: what you
changed, what the SWR/signal was before and after. Radio
problems are almost always one variable, and notes turn a
mystery into a ten-minute fix. When stuck, describe the symptom
precisely ("SWR 3:1 on 2 m, was 1.3:1 yesterday, nothing
moved") on a club forum or net — precise symptoms get precise
answers.

> **Check:** After any fix, re-verify the baseline: SWR
> reading, a successful repeater contact, a signal report.
> "It works" is a measurement, not a feeling.
